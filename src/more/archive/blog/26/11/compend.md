Recently, I've been using a social networking service called [compend](https://compend.org). The site is similar to platforms like Letterboxd, where the main purpose is logging, rating, and reviewing things. However, while most reviewing platforms focus on a specific type of media (ex. movies in the case of Letterboxd), compend allows reviewing of anything that has a Wikipedia page for it. This means being able to log and review abstract concepts and mundane objects, for example: *[Recycling](https://compend.org/item/Q132580)*, *[Cantaloupe](https://compend.org/item/Q477179)*, *[Egg timer](https://compend.org/item/Q1411787)*, the game show *[Wipeout](https://compend.org/item/Q52497)*, and the `7,268,634` other items with a Wikipedia article (not even counting items with an article in a language other than English, as those are available too).

compend began almost as a joke but overtime evolved into an actual thing. While a lot of reviews are one-liners, people saying "awww" to a cute cat breed, or new users logging into the site to mark objectively bad concepts such as *[Cancer](https://compend.org/item/Q12078)* a half-star (because we were all dying to know), long-form reviews still get written. There is even a section on the front page dedicated to longer reviews. Additionally, lists of items can be created. I'll return to those, they're important.

## User Types

compend's ["Style Guide"](https://compend.org/guide) mentions the site being built to fulfill the needs of two types of users: The Diarist and The Codifier. The culture of compend has evolved beyond the 2 types of users the site was designed for. Here are some common types of netizens that can be encountered on compend:

### The Diarist

To copy directly from the "Style Guide,"

> The Diarist chronically appraises experience. They log “*[Hello (Salutation)](https://compend.org/item/Q98815142)*” quite soon after having greeted someone. To the Diarist, **items are instances**. “*Hello (Salutation)*” will be logged approximately 4,000 times over the course of a decade. The Diarist, holidaying in *[Italy](https://compend.org/item/Q38)*, crouches over a *[Lenovo](https://compend.org/item/Q14799) [Trackpad](https://compend.org/item/Q20137)* to give Italy 3-and-a-half stars. Perhaps a meandering journal entry is written. **One could imagine the Diarist combing through a recursively shortened moment, never quite managing to log everything in it**.[^1]

**The Diarist** doesn't exist, kinda. Most users, rather than logging every encounter with an object, write a single review for an object and move on. There are exceptions: for instance diegetic meta pages (compend users log site changes on the page for *[Changelog](https://compend.org/item/Q255135)*, leave site suggestions/feedback on the page for [*Suggestion box](https://compend.org/item/Q2923114)*, or report bugs with the site on the page for *[Bug tracking system](https://compend.org/item/Q956086)*) or share scores for *[The New York Times Games](https://compend.org/item/Q124287090)* (mainly *[Wordle](https://compend.org/item/Q110367996)* and *[The New York Times Connections](https://compend.org/item/Q122207315)*), just to name a few examples. I'm not saying users who are by definition the diarist don't exist, I've just never encountered one. And *[If a tree falls in a forest and no one is around to hear it, does it make a sound?](https://compend.org/item/Q587736)* Most users who log the same item multiple times only do for certain items they find importance in.

With that said, compend is still frequently used as a diary. Traditional longer entries can still be found: in-depth reviews, diary entries, rants, venting, appreciation posts, etc. Said reviews are found in the ["Longform" tab on the home page](https://compend.org/?tab=digest). These longform entries are usually written on the page for the most prominent object in the writing (somewhat similar to [Everything2](https://everything2.com/)), but some users write on the page for the current date. However, compend also enables a more unique type of diary. It's time to talk about lists.

#### Lists

Lists are exactly what they sound like: **lists of items**. As of writing, two main types of lists are available: **standard** lists and **grouped** lists:

- **Standard lists** are exactly what they sound like: a list of items. Items can be added to a list and be reorganized. Additionally, rankings can be enabled, where a number is shown next to each item showing its rank, based on where the list author placed the item in the list.
- **Grouped lists** are lists sorted into groups, where groups can be named anything. Rankings aren't available for this list type.

Each item on a list can have a note attached to it, which allows the author of the list to provide commentary for each item, usually to add context. Some users intentionally avoid adding notes, as they enjoy both coming to their own conclusions when looking at other's lists, and having others come to their own conclusions on their lists. Comments are also available, which are used both for discussion of a list, but also to provide suggestions to the list author.

Lists are used for many different things: ranking objects in a category, collecting items for later purposes, centralizing related topics a user finds interesting, shitposting, or using lists as a diary.

These diary lists are unique. Rather than being writing focused, they focus on the items and emotions felt throughout the period (most commonly users create a diary list for each day, but some users make the period for a week). For instance, my day today would be represented as a list like this:
